Off-peak only¶

labllm.peak decides, for the fake and the real gateway alike, whether an off-peak-only call may go out now. Four choices cost something if reversed.

The hours come from the price entry, read by our own code. A model's off-peak hours are the off_peak_pricing block of its entry in LiteLLM's pinned table, carried on Price.off_peak; a test hands the fake the same block. OffPeak reads it as LiteLLM's cost function does, since the plain install, and so the fake, has no LiteLLM. The cost: a pin that changes how LiteLLM reads the block can split our hours from its prices, so moving the pin means re-reading its _is_off_peak. A model with no off-peak hours is unaffected.

Peak travels as an error with a time. The reply is {"error": "peak_hours", "message": ..., "until": <epoch seconds>}. The client waits on its own clock until then, and at least a second after it sent, then sends again; the caller never sees it. A client clock behind the gateway's sends early and is told again.

A cache hit is answered in peak. No call reaches the provider, so an off-peak-only pass rerun in peak replays its finished units at once. A peak reply is not an exchange.

The margin is a refusal window. A call is refused when a peak hour starts within peak_margin_minutes of it (5 by default), and until skips off-peak gaps shorter than the margin. Whether DeepSeek bills by a call's start or end is not established; the margin keeps a call clear of the boundary either way, unless the call outlasts it.

Consequences¶

  • Meter.stop wakes every call waiting out peak hours. A pass stops its meter when it stops, so a waiting unit ends unfinished with the stop's reason and sends nothing.
  • Clock.sleep_until(t, wake) joins the clock seam. FakeClock moves to the later of now and t, so threads waiting for one moment wake together.
  • A unit's time per pass includes its wait.
  • The check runs before each attempt's ceiling hold, retries included, once the call's turn at its rate limit comes.
